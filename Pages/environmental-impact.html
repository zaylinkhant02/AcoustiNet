The Acoustic Environment

Urban monitoring models across key townships in Yangon confirm that roadside sound pressure regularly surpasses 75 dBA during high-volume periods. This constant sonic footprint penetrates surrounding urban canopies, fracturing animal vocal communication channels and decreasing the ecological value of local natural sanctuaries.

How City Noise Disrupts Our Natural Ecosystems

Constant traffic and horn sounds do more than bother people; they also deeply harm urban wildlife. Verified records from the European Environment Agency (EEA, 2025) show that noise creates an invisible wall that alters how animals hunt, hide, and breed in city green spaces.

"A global study on wildlife acoustics (Francis et al., 2011) proved that low-frequency road noise completely covers up animal calls. This forces larger bird species with deeper songs to abandon their city habitats entirely."

By tracking these patterns, designing quieter green corridors helps local wildlife can safely communicate and live normally again.

70% Less Hearing Area

Studies by Barber et al. (2009) reveal that traffic noise shrinks the clean zone where predators can hear food or danger by 70%.

Altered Song Cycles

City birds are forced to change their natural pitch or sing late at night just to find mates over the heavy roar of nearby roads.

Urban parks and wildlife corridors suffer when loud traffic drowns out natural animal soundscapes.

How Noise Indirectly Harms Trees and Plants

While plants do not have ears, loud city noise still harms them. Verified research from the Royal Society B (Francis et al., 2012) shows that noise changes entire forest layouts. This happens because loud sounds scare away the birds and small animals that plants depend on to survive.

"When noise drives away helpful animals, plants lose their partners. Mice and birds stop moving seeds around, which stops new trees from growing in noisy areas."

Our monitoring system maps these quiet patterns to show exactly where trees and plants are losing their animal partners due to heavy street traffic.
