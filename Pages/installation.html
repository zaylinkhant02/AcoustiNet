Mounting Guide

Device Installation

This page explains how to safely mount your finished weather box in outdoor public spaces. Learn how to pick the right spot on a wall or pole, secure the box using strong metal brackets, and safely route the 5V power cord to an outlet so your noise tracker stays up and running permanently.

Step 1

Selecting the Installation Spot

Vertical Height Requirements

Before drilling any holes, use a tape measure to find a spot that is high enough off the ground to keep the equipment secure from accidental bumps, vandalism, or curious animals.

  • Minimum Height: Choose a location that is at least 8 feet (2.4 meters) above the ground level.
  • Maximum Height: Do not exceed 12 feet (3.6 meters) high, as you will need to safely reach the box with a standard ladder for occasional cleaning or maintenance checks.
  • Surface Type: Look for flat, solid surfaces like brick walls, thick wooden poles, or concrete pillars that can support the weight of the box during heavy wind storms.

Power Outlet Proximity

Since the device uses a 5V power adapter rather than solar panels, your choice of location is strictly limited by the distance to the nearest electrical wall plug.

  • Cord Measurement: Measure the exact length of your 5V power adapter cord before mounting the box. The box must be close enough so the cord sags slightly and is not pulled completely straight.
  • Outlet Protection: The wall outlet must be an outdoor-rated, waterproof electrical box with a plastic cover that closes completely over the plugged-in adapter to block rain.
  • Extension Cords: Do not use temporary indoor extension cords to bridge the gap. If the cord cannot reach a safe outdoor outlet, pick a new mounting spot entirely.

Acoustic Path Rules

To collect correct data, the microphone sensor needs an unblocked path to the surrounding air. Sound waves bounce off large objects, which can alter the noise readings.

  • Clear Space: Keep the weather box at least 3 feet away from deep wall corners, large overhanging roof eaves, or thick tree branches.
  • Avoid Local Noise Sources: Do not mount the box directly next to air conditioning exhaust fans, spinning wind vents, or vibrating gutters. These objects make local sounds that will ruin your general neighborhood noise tracking numbers.
Step 2

Attaching the Box to the Wall

1. Fix the Mounting Brackets to the Box

Before climbing up the ladder, you must attach the exterior metal mounting ears to the plastic weather box. This gives you a clear flange to screw through when attaching the system to the wall.

  • Flip the weather box over onto its front side so the back face points upward.
  • Line up the four stainless steel mounting brackets with the factory screw holes on the four corners of the box.
  • Insert the short machine screws through the brackets and into the box holes. Tighten them fully with a handheld screwdriver so the brackets do not swing or rattle.

2. Mark the Drill Points on the Wall

To keep the box straight and level, mark your exact drill holes directly onto the brick or wood surface first.

  • Hold the plastic box up against the wall at your chosen installation spot.
  • Place a small bubble level on top of the box to ensure it sits perfectly straight. If the box tilts, rain could slide into the microphone hole.
  • Take a pencil or a thin marker and draw small dots inside the screw loops of the metal brackets onto the wall surface. Set the box back down in a safe spot.

3. Drill Holes and Insert Plastic Anchors

If you are mounting the device onto a hard surface like brick, concrete, or stone, you must use plastic wall anchors to prevent the screws from pulling out under heavy stress.

  • Attach a masonry drill bit to your power drill that matches the thickness of your plastic anchors.
  • Drill a straight hole into each of the marked marks. Push the drill inward until the hole depth matches the full length of the anchor.
  • Clear any loose dust out of the holes, then push the plastic anchors inside. Use a hammer to tap the anchors lightly until their outer rims sit completely flush against the wall surface.

Note for Wooden Surfaces: If you are mounting the box onto a soft wooden pole or fence board, you do not need to drill large holes or use plastic anchors. You can drive the mounting screws straight through the brackets into the wood securely.

4. Mount and Lock the Box Framework

The final step fastens the device framework permanently to the wall structure using heavy-duty outdoor screws.

  • Lift the weather box back into place, matching up the metal brackets with the installed wall anchors.
  • Insert long outdoor wood or wall screws through the brackets and into the anchors.
  • Drive the screws inward using your power drill or screwdriver. Tighten them firmly until the metal brackets compress hard against the wall face. Pull gently on the box to confirm it does not shift or wiggle.
