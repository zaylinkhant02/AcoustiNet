Our Tools

Hardware Plan

To track city noise accurately, we use simple physical tools built to last. Learn about the physical parts, sound meters, and power tools we select to keep our monitoring setup active and safe in open public spaces.

The Pieces

Main Parts

Sound Sensor

This part listens to the surrounding noise. It turns the physical sound waves into digital numbers that measure loudness levels.

Main Computer Board

This is the small brain of the device. It takes the loudness numbers from the sensor, reads them, and sends them to the display screen.

5V Power Adapter

This plug connects the device to a standard wall outlet. It changes the wall electricity into a safe 5V stream to keep the tools running constantly without batteries.

Weather Box

A strong plastic case that holds all the parts safely inside. It keeps the fragile electronic pieces dry from heavy rain and wind.

Connection Setup & Assembly Steps

Step 1: Wire the Sensor Pins

Use three separate plastic jumper wires to link the sound sensor directly to the main computer board. Push the female wire tips firmly onto the metal pins until they seat completely and do not wiggle loose.

Sound Sensor Pin Name Computer Board Pin Name What This Wire Carries
VCC 5V Electricity to turn the sensor on
GND GND Ground connection to complete the circuit
OUT (or A0) A0 The volume signal numbers sent from the microphone

Step 2: Smooth Out the Power Supply

Small variations in electricity can mess up the noise readings. To fix this, add a tiny electronic helper called a capacitor directly between the power and ground lines:

  • Take a small 0.1µF capacitor and connect its two metal legs directly across the sensor's VCC and GND lines.
  • This part works like a temporary storage tank that absorbs quick electrical drops, making sure the sensor receives steady, clean power.

Step 3: Thread and Lock the Power Adapter

Do not plug the power adapter into the wall yet. First, you must pass the cord into the weather box correctly to keep rain out:

  • Unscrew the round plastic cap on the water-tight grip plug (cable gland) at the bottom of your weather box.
  • Push the small USB end of the 5V power adapter wire through the outer cap, then through the rubber center ring, and into the box.
  • Plug the USB end directly into the matching power slot on your computer board.
  • Screw the outer plastic cap back onto the box hole tightly. The internal rubber ring will squeeze around the wire to block any water drops from following the cord inside.

Step 4: Screw the Boards into the Enclosure

Leaving loose parts inside the box can break the wires when the device is moved or shaken by strong winds. Secure everything down mechanically:

  • Align the small holes on the corners of the computer board with the raised plastic pegs inside the weather box.
  • Use four small metal screws to secure the board tightly down onto the pegs. Do not overtighten, or the plastic board might crack.

Step 5: Mount and Seal the Microphone Tip

The sound sensor needs a clear path to hear the air outside, but the circuit board must stay completely dry.

  • Find the round 10mm hole cut into the very bottom face of your weather box.
  • Push the silver tip of the microphone sensor through this hole from the inside so it points down at the ground. This upside-down position prevents falling rain from collecting inside the microphone frame.

Important Sealing Rule: Squeeze a thick ring of waterproof silicone glue around the outside edge of the microphone tip where it meets the plastic box. Let the glue sit and dry for two hours. This creates an airtight seal that blocks damp air and heavy rain from sneaking inside the box gaps.

Checking the Tools

Testing the Setup

Step 1

Power Check

Plug the device into the wall outlet. Look closely at the small computer board to make sure a tiny red light turns on. This shows electricity is flowing inside.

Step 2

Sound Check

Make a loud noise like a clap near the sensor. Watch your main screen to see if the noise numbers go up instantly, then drop back down when it is quiet again.

Step 3

Box Check

Close the plastic weather box tightly. Pour a small splash of water on top of the box to confirm no water leaks inside onto the wires and parts.

Keeping Tools Working

Taking Care of the Tools

Cleaning the Box

Wipe the outside plastic box with a dry cloth every month. Remove dirt or leaves that cover the bottom sensor hole so it can still hear perfectly.

Checking the Wires

Look at the power cord and adapter regularly. Make sure the wires are not bent, cut, or pulled out of the wall outlet tightly.

Replacing Old Parts

If the red light on the computer board stops shining or numbers stop changing on the screen, swap the broken piece for a fresh spare part.
